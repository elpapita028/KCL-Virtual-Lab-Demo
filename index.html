Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL) Virtual Lab

To study and verify Kirchhoff’s Current Law

Aim

To verify Kirchhoff’s Current Law by connecting a power supply and three ammeters to a resistive circuit, and showing that the current entering the junction equals the sum of the currents leaving it (I₁ = I₂ + I₃).

Apparatus: variable DC power supply, three ammeters (A1, A2, A3), three resistors (R₁, R₂, R₃) and connecting wires (virtual).

Theory

Kirchhoff’s Current Law is based on conservation of charge: charge cannot pile up at a node, so the current entering a node equals the current leaving it.

ΣIin = ΣIout  or  ΣI = 0

In this circuit R₂ and R₃ are in parallel and that pair is in series with R₁. The current I₁ flows through R₁, reaches the junction and splits into I₂ (through R₂) and I₃ (through R₃).

Req = R₁ + (R₂ × R₃)/(R₂ + R₃)  ,  I₁ = V / Req
I₂ = I₁ × R₃/(R₂ + R₃)  ,  I₃ = I₁ × R₂/(R₂ + R₃)
I₁ = I₂ + I₃

Pretest

Procedure

  1. Make connections. Drag from one node to another to draw a wire. Click a wire to delete it.
    • Power supply to circuit: 1–9 and 2–10.
    • Ammeter A1: 3–11 and 4–12.
    • Ammeter A2: 5–13 and 6–14.
    • Ammeter A3: 7–15 and 8–16.
  2. Click CHECK. Correct wires turn green and wrong ones red. Fix any mistakes.
  3. Click POWER to switch the supply on.
  4. Set V, R₁, R₂ and R₃ with the sliders, or press a Set button to load the values below.
  5. Read A1 (I₁), A2 (I₂) and A3 (I₃). Click RECORD to add the reading to the table.
  6. Repeat for all five sets of values.
  7. Compare I₁ with I₂ + I₃ in the table and graph. They should match.
